# Ostium starter party: art director review

Bar: Darkest Dungeon 2 roster. Heavy, grounded adventurers, instant silhouette read, posture that tells you who they are, layered costume with story details. Severity: S1 = blocks owner review, S2 = clearly wrong, S3 = polish.

## Shared problems (every figure)

1. S1 Boots read as socks or slippers. `boot()` is one rounded blob: no heel block, no instep, no toe, no sole thickness, and the shaft starts as a separate block halfway up the shin with a hard seam where the leg tube meets it. At 190 px tall the feet read as stumps. This alone makes every figure look like a puppet. Build instead: shaft to the knee (or a set height) in one shape with a calf swell and Achilles curve; a separate dark sole with a raised arch and a stacked heel block; a toe cap seam, a stitched welt, ankle creases, an optional strap and buckle; for armoured legs a sabaton of overlapping lames with rivets and a pointed toe cap.
2. S1 Turned-down boot cuffs (A and B duelists, C duelist) are drawn as rounded quads and read as pillows or tyres around the knee. A cavalier boot top is a flared bucket that rises higher over the front of the knee than the back, with fold lines.
3. S1 Ears are drawn as a large flat disc at the middle of the head. In every face it reads as a headphone cup. A 3/4 ear sits behind the jaw hinge, is about a third of the head height and narrow, with a helix line, and is mostly covered by hair or coif.
4. S2 Hands are too small and too thin to read: fingers are 4 to 5 px tubes, palms are smaller than the face's nose-to-chin. DD2 hands are big (a fist is about half a head). `fist()` reads as a grey lump with three hairlines; there are no curled finger segments, no thumb shape, no gauntlet cuff or knuckle plate. Rebuild as palm block + four stacked curled finger segments with crease shadows + a thumb that wraps over them, optional gauntlet knuckle plate and finger lames.
5. S2 Knee and elbow cops read as balls. `poleyn()` is one small rounded shield, about 60 percent of leg width; dirA's couter is a plain ellipse. Make them faceted cops with a ridge, an articulated lame above and below, and a side wing, sized to the joint.
6. S2 The ostium badge is a grey ellipse with a slash on every figure and reads as a ball or a button. It is the game's emblem: make it an arched lead door token with a dark doorway, on a cord loop. The badge is missing entirely on A and C Elementalist, and the red rubric strip is missing on B and C Elementalist.
7. S2 dirA and dirB rim light is a 5 px, 75 percent opaque pale outline on every shape. It reads as a sticker halo or neon outline, not a cool fill. Rim should be 2 to 3 px and about half opacity.
8. S3 Noses are hard triangles with sharp tips (most visible on A Elementalist): reads cartoony. Faces lack a jaw underplane and an under-brow shadow, so the head reads as a flat cut-out.

### Recurring cause, fixed at the source
`B.fist(f, c, ang)` took a raw angle and could only draw the thumb on one side (screen angle ang - 90 degrees). To get the thumb toward a haft's head, an author's only option was to spin the fist by 180 degrees. That points the knuckles back into the forearm. It caused the original Thrall error and two of the failures above (the Brewer's hip fist and the Smith's hammer fist). The other grip failures came from typing an angle that ignored where the elbow was.
- `body.js` `fist()`: new `o.flip` mirrors the hand so the thumb can sit on either side without turning the hand round. The header comment now states the convention.
- `body.js` `grip(fig, c, elbow, thumbAng, s, col, o)` and `gripAngle(...)`: they work the fist angle out from the elbow and the object's direction. The back of the hand faces the elbow, the object runs along the knuckle row, and the thumb side is chosen by flipping. If the forearm meets the object more than `o.maxBend` (35 degrees) off square, the hand turns no further than that and the render prints `grip <name>: ... move the elbow`. `o.inline` is for sword and tong grips, where the object continues the forearm. All current grips render with no warning. dirA, dirB and dirC are unchanged because `flip` defaults to off.
- `B.head` always aims the eyes along the head's own axis, so gaze is set only by the head angle. That is why two of the gaze misses went through. Added to the checklist.

### Still open
- The Brewer stands upright under a copper still on his back. His centre of mass is still over his feet, so this is not wrong, but a slight forward lean would sell the weight.
- The Thrall's header says his weight is sunk on the back foot, but both legs are nearly straight and evenly planted. That is physically fine; it does not show the stated intent.
- The poppet faces the camera, not the Cunning Woman. She is stabbing it, not reading it, so it passes, and turning it would lose its face at game size.
- The Bulwark's mace grip is a deliberate 51 degree diagonal across the palm (allowed with maxBend 40). It would be worth having an artist look at it.
- Hand chirality is only modelled through which side the thumb is on. `fist()` draws one hand shape, and a far left hand is drawn as its mirror when the thumb needs it.

## Rig conversion (2026-11-22)

The Duelist, Elementalist, Alchemist, Oracle, Hexweaver, Drowned Thrall and Smith now carry joints and bones like the Bulwark. Shapes, colours and positions are unchanged. Every figure was re-rendered with `render.js` and compared with its `render_rig.js` check.png (mean absolute difference, RGB over a mid grey). It was also compared with the render from before the conversion.

How shapes that span a joint are handled: `split()` and `side()` in `dirAC.js` (exported, and used by `dirAC2.js` too). A shape drawn as one outline across a joint is drawn once. Examples are a tall boot from knee to toe, a trouser tube from hip to mid-shin, a skin tube through the elbow, and a robe from shoulder to hem. The same drawing is then handed to each bone, clipped to that bone's side of the joint. Both sides also own a disk of about half the limb width round the pivot, and the child copy is drawn on top. When the child turns, its round end covers the parent's, so no gap opens. In the rest pose the copies coincide. The only trace is a slightly darker antialiased edge inside the overlap disk (at most 0.011 mean difference from the old render). Robes and habits are cut at the rope belt. The skirt copy reaches up under the torso copy, and the torso copy stops under the belt, which hides the cut.
